DIY vs Professional Purifier Service: The Honest Truth

Some tasks are safe at home. Others quietly void your warranty. Here's exactly where the line is.

Vikram Rao

Chief Technician, AquaPure

Every month I answer the same question on support calls: "Can't I just fix this myself?" Sometimes the answer is a cheerful yes. Other times it's a "please don't" that saves you a ₹4,000 repair bill.

Here's my honest, field-tested breakdown of what's safe to DIY, what isn't, and the exact moment you should call a technician.

Safe to DIY at Home

TaskDifficultyToolsRisk
Exterior wipe-down & dustingVery easyDamp clothNone
Pre-filter rinsingEasyNoneLow
Clearing drain line kinksEasyNoneLow
TDS meter spot-checksEasyTDS penNone

These keep your purifier healthy between visits and cost nothing. But notice what's missing: anything that opens the water path inside the unit.

Leave These to the Pros

  • Filter / Membrane swaps

    Air can enter the sealed path during a swap, which then requires a system flush only service technicians can do properly. Wrong seating also voids warranty.

  • UV lamp replacement

    A damaged quartz sleeve is nearly invisible — and the reason water stops being disinfected. We check it, you shouldn't.

  • Electrical / SMPS issues

    Water + electronics = risk to you and your family. High-voltage parts are certified, not guessed at.

  • Booster pump problems

    Wrong pressure calibration damages the membrane — the most expensive part in the machine.

The 3-Minute DIY Checklist

  1. 1

    Smell the output. A musty note usually means the carbon filter is exhausted.

  2. 2

    Check TDS weekly. A sudden jump above 100 ppm above your baseline = membrane time.

  3. 3

    Time your recovery. If filling a 1L bottle now takes noticeably longer, call us today, not next week.

"If you can see water pooling anywhere it shouldn't, or you can hear the pump cycling on and off rapidly — that's not a DIY moment. That's a 'turn it off and call' moment."

— Vikram, AquaPure

When to Replace Your RO Membrane (Easy Checklist)

Bad taste, slow recovery, rising TDS — here are the five signs your membrane has finished its job, plus how to test at home.

Sana Kapoor

Water Quality Specialist

The RO membrane is the heart of your purifier — and the most expensive part to replace. Most families replace it too early (wasting money) or too late (drinking poor water). Here's how to know.

The 5 Warning Signs

  • 1. Taste & smell change

    A salty, bitter or plastic-like note in the output water is a classic membrane-end-of-life signal.

  • 2. Slower recovery

    If a 1L bottle now takes noticeably longer to fill, the membrane is fouling and losing throughput.

  • 3. TDS creeping up

    A sudden jump of 100+ ppm over your baseline usually means the membrane can no longer reject dissolved solids.

  • 4. Frequent reject-water run

    A higher waste-to-clean ratio is a sign the membrane is struggling.

  • 5. Age — the silent one

    Even without symptoms, most membranes last 2–3 years. Beyond that, replace proactively.

How to Test at Home

Pick up an inexpensive TDS pen. Compare the purifier's output to your tap water. Rejection should be roughly 90%+ on membranes in good health. Below that, it's time to book a swap.

"A TDS pen costs less than a cup of coffee at a café and removes all the guesswork."

— Sana, AquaPure

Monsoon Water Care: Why Filters Need Extra Attention

Heavy rains spike turbidity and bacterial load in groundwater. Three simple steps keep your family safe all monsoon.

Arjun Mehta

Service Lead, West Zone

Monsoon means relief from heat — and a quiet spike in water problems. Rainwater washes surface silt, organic matter and microbes into wells, overhead tanks and pipelines. Your filters are the first line of defence.

What Changes in the Rainy Season

  • Turbidity rises

    Sediment and silt clog pre-filters faster — check and rinse them twice as often.

  • Bacterial load climbs

    Surface run-off can carry bacteria. UV lamps must be healthy — replace any lamp older than a year.

  • Tank contamination

    Check your overhead tank inlet for gaps and get it cleaned if you missed last year's schedule.

The 3-Step Monsoon Routine

  1. 1

    Rinse pre-filters fortnightly. A clogged pre-filter starves the rest of the system.

  2. 2

    Sanitise before the first rain. A quick UV+RO flush removes any biofilm from the storage tank.

  3. 3

    Schedule a monsoon checkup. One visit now beats an emergency call in August.

"90% of post-monsoon repair calls we take were preventable with a single pre-monsoon visit."

RO vs UV vs Gravity: The 2026 Decision Guide

Your water source, TDS and budget decide the answer. We break down every trade-off in plain language.

Meera Iyer

Product Consultant

Choosing a purifier shouldn't need a chemistry degree. Start with your water source, check the TDS, and the answer usually becomes obvious.

Quick Rule of Thumb

Your waterTDSPick
Municipal / tap0–300UV purifier
Borewell / hard water300+RO purifier
No power supplyAnyGravity purifier

The Three Technologies

  • RO — Reverse Osmosis

    Pushes water through a semi-permeable membrane, removing dissolved solids, heavy metals and hardness. Best for borewell and high-TDS water. Some water is wasted as reject.

  • UV — Ultraviolet

    Inactivates bacteria and viruses with UV light, keeping the minerals in. Ideal for clean municipal water. Needs electricity.

  • Gravity

    Filters by gravity through UF and carbon — no power, no water waste. Great for power-cut areas and small families.

"The right purifier is the one matched to your water test — not the one with the fanciest logo."

— Meera, AquaPure
